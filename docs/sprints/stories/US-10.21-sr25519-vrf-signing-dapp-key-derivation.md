## Why EPIC-10 owns it — provisionally

[EPIC-10](../epics/EPIC-10.md) is the epic that lets a dApp *"approve connections, **signatures** and
transactions"*, and the title says **for dApp**, so a new dApp-facing signing capability belongs
there.

**Provisional, because the title alone cannot settle it.** If this turns out to be keyring/crypto
machinery with no dApp-facing surface, [EPIC-2](../epics/EPIC-2.md) (Core Platform Engines) is the
better home; if it is really about derivation paths, [US-3.7](US-3.7-account-derivation-custom-path-child-accounts.md)
is the neighbour. Nothing in the repo mentions **sr25519 VRF** today.

## Open questions

1. **Who asks?** Does a dApp request a VRF signature over a payload, or request that the wallet
   *derive* a per-dApp key and sign with it? Those are different surfaces and different consent
   models.
2. **What does the user approve?** VRF output is not human-readable. Whatever the prompt shows, it
   has to satisfy the [US-5.15](US-5.15-signing-prompt-mode-confusion.md) rule from #5042 — a
   signature may only be produced over the artefact the prompt displayed.
3. **Which key?** A derived per-dApp key, or an existing account key? If derived, the derivation path
   and its recoverability matter.
4. **Priority.** No label, no assignee, no milestone — `P3` here is a placeholder, not a decision.
