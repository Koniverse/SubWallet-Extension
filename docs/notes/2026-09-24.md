## A. Research ask — USDC / ETH (Ethereum) ↔ dTAO

### The ask

Raised 2026-09-24: **look into swapping USDC or ETH on Ethereum directly into dTAO** (a Bittensor
subnet alpha token).

**There is no written specification behind it** — no issue, no requirement, nothing that states
what the route should do or who would carry it. Everything below is read out of this repository and
describes only what the code can and cannot do today. Nothing here is a requirement, and the ask
itself has not been checked against anything outside the repo
([LESSONS §69](../LESSONS.md): an unverified claim stays unverified until something corroborates it).

### What the wallet can already do

Three of the four pieces exist and shipped:

| Piece | Where | Story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ethereum in-chain swap** (USDC ↔ ETH and the rest of the L1 book) | `swap-service/handler/uniswap-handler.ts`, `kyber-handler.ts` | [US-11.3](../sprints/stories/US-11.3-in-wallet-swap-via-uniswap.md), [US-11.4](../sprints/stories/US-11.4-in-wallet-swap-via-kyberswap.md) |
| **dTAO swap** — TAO ↔ alpha and alpha ↔ alpha across subnets, on the per-subnet AMM | `swap-service/handler/bittensor-handler.ts` (`SwapProviderId.BITTENSOR`) | [US-11.7](../sprints/stories/US-11.7-in-wallet-bittensor-dtao-swap.md), FR-109, shipped **1.3.78** (#4899) |
| **Multi-hop routing** — compose a swap with a bridge and track each hop, resumable | `swap-service/index.ts`, `utils.ts` (`DynamicSwapType.SWAP` / `BRIDGE`) | [US-11.8](../sprints/stories/US-11.8-cross-chain-swap-routing.md) |
| **A bridge between Ethereum and Bittensor** | — | **nothing** |

### The gap is one hop, and it is the middle one

A USDC(ethereum) → alpha route is, at minimum, **swap → bridge → swap**: sell USDC for something
bridgeable on Ethereum, move it to Bittensor, buy alpha with TAO. The first and third legs are
covered. **The middle leg does not exist in this tree.** Every bridge under
`balance-service/transfer/xcm/` was checked:

- **`bittensorBridge/`** — the closest, and it is not it. `getSubtensorEvmtoBittensorExtrinsic()`
  calls the precompile at `0x0000000000000000000000000000000000000800`, and the reverse direction is
  a substrate `balances.transferKeepAlive` to an `evmToSs58()` address. **Both legs are native TAO,
  between the Bittensor substrate chain and Subtensor EVM** — one ecosystem, one asset. It is
  #4901's bridge (1.3.78), not an Ethereum one.
- **`snowBridge.ts`** — the only Ethereum↔substrate bridge here, and it lands on Polkadot / Asset
  Hub, not Bittensor.
- **`acrossBridge/`, `polygonBridge.ts`, `posBridge.ts`, `availBridge.ts`** — EVM-side, none reaches
  Bittensor.

So today the router cannot build this route because no provider can quote the hop that leaves
Ethereum for Bittensor. That is the thing to research: **who carries value between Ethereum and
Bittensor, and on what terms.**

### Questions research has to answer, in order

1. **Is there a bridge at all?** Named, live, with an API the wallet could call. If the answer is a
   third-party swap desk rather than a bridge (the SimpleSwap / Optimex shape —
   [US-11.5](../sprints/stories/US-11.5-in-wallet-swap-via-simpleswap.md),
   [US-11.9](../sprints/stories/US-11.9-in-wallet-swap-via-optimex.md)), that is a **different
   integration** from a routed multi-hop and probably a smaller one.
2. **Which asset crosses?** TAO itself, or a wrapped/bridged representation? If the bridge lands
   TAO on Subtensor EVM rather than the substrate chain, `bittensorBridge` already covers the last
   leg and the route is **four hops**, not three.
3. **Does the router actually run three legs end to end?** `processStepsToPathActions()` builds
   `path` as a list, so nothing in the type structurally caps it at two — but US-11.8 was written
   and tested around **Swap→Bridge / Bridge→Swap**. Whether a third leg quotes, prices and resumes
   correctly is **unverified either way**; do not assume it does or does not.
   **Answered later the same day — no, not with two swaps.**
   [`getLatestQuoteFromSwapRequest`](../../packages/extension-base/src/services/swap-service/index.ts#L230)
   takes `path.find(… === DynamicSwapType.SWAP)` — the **first** SWAP action — and quotes only
   that one; every other action is treated as a bridge. The path itself comes from the backend
   (`subwalletApiSdk.swapApi.findAvailablePath`), not from the extension. A **SWAP → SWAP** route is
   therefore not quotable today, and changing that is backend *and* extension work.
4. **Where does the alpha end up?** `bittensor-handler.ts` reads `fromAsset.metadata?.netuid` and
   treats the native token as netuid 0, so the destination alpha has to be a chain-list asset with a
   `netuid`. A route into an arbitrary subnet needs that asset to exist first.
5. **What does it cost, and is it honest?** Three legs means three fees and two intermediate
   balances. [US-11.8](../sprints/stories/US-11.8-cross-chain-swap-routing.md)
   already carries the rule that a half-completed multi-hop must be resumable and never silently
   lost; a longer route makes that harder, not easier.

### Three ways

**Question 1 now has an answer from outside the repo.** On 2026-09-16 TAO.com announced *"USDC to
dTAO Crosschain swaps. Now LIVE … Directly from Subnets into USDC (Or ETH) and vice versa"*, with a
follow-up saying *"Our Intents are powered via LayerZero"* ([post](https://x.com/taodotcom/status/2100230762087760367),
read from a screenshot). So the route exists, and someone is running it. The three ways below
are the three different ways SubWallet could offer it too.

#### At a glance

| | Way 1 — SimpleSwap + Bittensor AMM | Way 2 — TAO.com as a provider | Way 3 — vTAO over LayerZero OFT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Route** | USDC/ETH → TAO on SimpleSwap, then TAO → alpha on-chain | One order; TAO.com delivers dTAO | USDC → vTAO → bridge → unwrap → EVM→substrate → alpha |
| **Legs the wallet runs** | 2 | 1 | 5 |
| **User signatures** | 2 (1 with One-sign, see below) | 1 | 4–5 (fewer with One-sign) |
| **Time to alpha** | Minutes (SimpleSwap is an off-chain desk) | Seconds, per TAO.com | Minutes (LayerZero delivery + 4 txs) |
| **What already exists** | Both providers: SimpleSwap has TAO since #3855 (1.3.11); Bittensor handler since 1.3.78 | Nothing; would be a new provider next to SimpleSwap / Optimex | Last two legs only (`bittensorBridge`, Bittensor handler) |
| **Contracts to deploy** | None | None | None — vTAO OFT is deployed and audited |
| **Liquidity to add** | None | None — TAO.com carries it | None for the bridge; depends on someone else's USDC/vTAO pool |
| **Blocked on** | Router SWAP→SWAP (backend + extension) | A partner API — **none found publicly** | Router SWAP→…→SWAP, a new bridge type, a new unwrap step |
| **Where funds can stop mid-route** | 1 point (as TAO) | 0 for the wallet (escrow refunds on the protocol side) | 4 points |
| **Size** | Medium | Small–medium once an API exists | Large |

#### Why TAO.com needs one signature and the wallet needs several

The wallet today **orchestrates every leg itself, with the user's key, on each chain**. Every leg
is its own transaction. Protocols that get this down to one signature move the destination-side
work off the user's key. Two shapes, both seen in the wild:

- **Intent + solver** — what TAO.com's *"Intents … via LayerZero"* and *"settle in seconds"* point
  to. The user signs one order on Ethereum and USDC is escrowed. A solver (a market maker) pays out
  alpha from **its own inventory** on Bittensor straight away, LayerZero carries the proof of fill
  back, and escrow releases the USDC to the solver. The legs still happen, but later, and it is the
  solver that pays for them. *This is inferred from the post and from how intents generally work;
  TAO.com has published no technical docs that were found.*
- **Bridge-and-call** — what TaoFi (Subnet 10) does, open source at
  [TaoFi-0x/taofi-swap](https://github.com/TaoFi-0x/taofi-swap). One source-chain transaction
  (`lifiSwapBridgeAndCall`) swaps via LiFi, bridges USDC over a Hyperlane Warp Route, prepays
  destination gas, and triggers a Hyperlane **Interchain Account** on Bittensor EVM that calls
  `swapAndStake` (USDC → TAO on a Uniswap v3 pool, then stake through the StakingV2 precompile).
  It needs no solver inventory but waits on the bridge, and the user ends up with an ERC-20
  `AlphaToken`, **not native stake on their coldkey**, which SubWallet would not show as a staking
  position.

A wallet cannot get to one signature on its own. It needs either a contract doing the calling
(bridge-and-call) or a solver with capital (intent). That is why Way 2 is the only way here with
one signature.

#### Way 1 — SimpleSwap (USDC/ETH → TAO) + Bittensor AMM (TAO → alpha)

**What it is.** Chain two providers that already ship. SimpleSwap turns USDC/ETH on Ethereum into
TAO on Bittensor (added in [#3855](https://github.com/Koniverse/SubWallet-Extension/issues/3855), PR
#3939, 2024-12-20: *"Support swap TAO on SimpleSwap"*, released as 1.3.11). The Bittensor
handler then turns TAO into subnet alpha. **Chainflip does not list TAO**, so SimpleSwap is the
only provider for the first leg.

**What has to be built.**

| # | Work | Where | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Router emits and quotes **SWAP → SWAP** | backend `findAvailablePath` + [swap-service/index.ts:230](../../packages/extension-base/src/services/swap-service/index.ts#L230) | Only the first SWAP is quoted today (question 3) |
| 2 | Track the SimpleSwap order by `id` | new step after [simpleswap-handler.ts:207](../../packages/extension-base/src/services/swap-service/handler/simpleswap-handler.ts#L207) | The handler creates the exchange, sends the deposit and keeps `id`. **Nothing ever checks the order again** |
| 3 | Drive leg 2 from the **amount actually received** | same, plus the TODO at [simpleswap-handler.ts:255](../../packages/extension-base/src/services/swap-service/handler/simpleswap-handler.ts#L255) | The exchange is created with `fixed: false` ([line 73](../../packages/extension-base/src/services/swap-service/handler/simpleswap-handler.ts#L73)), a floating rate. `amountTo` is an estimate and only a >5% drop is rejected |
| 4 | Re-quote TAO → alpha at arrival and submit with a **price limit** | Bittensor handler (`swapStakeLimit`) / [tao.ts:830](../../packages/extension-base/src/services/earning-service/handlers/native-staking/tao.ts#L830) (`addStakeLimit`) | Alpha's price moves during the minutes SimpleSwap takes |
| 5 | Make One-sign wait for an off-chain leg (below) | [Extension.ts:6021-6068](../../packages/extension-base/src/koni/background/handlers/Extension.ts#L6021-L6068) | Today it does not wait at all for a SWAP leg |
| 6 | Resume unfinished processes when the background restarts | new | The One-sign loop lives only in memory |

**One-sign (`loopSubmit`) today.** `handleSubmitProcessTransaction` (the *Allow one sign* setting)
lets a password account sign step 1 and have the rest auto-approved from the unlocked keyring.
**No key is stored anywhere, and none should be.** It will fail on this route in four places:

1. **It only waits after an XCM step.** The wait branch is gated on
   `rs.extrinsicType === ExtrinsicType.TRANSFER_XCM` ([line 6033](../../packages/extension-base/src/koni/background/handlers/Extension.ts#L6033)).
   A SimpleSwap step is `SWAP`, so the stake step fires straight away, with no TAO yet. Even the XCM
   wait is capped: `setTimeout(resolve, 60 * 1000)` ([line 6035](../../packages/extension-base/src/koni/background/handlers/Extension.ts#L6035))
   resolves after a minute whether funds arrived or not.
2. **"Funds arrived" compares the whole balance.** `BigN(rs.value).gte(targetAmount)`
   ([line 6041](../../packages/extension-base/src/koni/background/handlers/Extension.ts#L6041)) is
   already true if the wallet held TAO before. For a swap, `targetAmount` is also the *input*
   amount (`quote.fromAmount`), not the TAO expected out. It should compare a delta against a
   pre-send snapshot, with the SimpleSwap order status as the primary signal.
3. **Later steps reuse the original quote.** Each step re-enters `handleSwapStep({...data})` with
   the quote from the start, so the stake amount is the estimate, not what arrived.
4. **The loop is in memory.** Chrome runs MV3 with a service worker. If the browser closes, the
   worker is suspended or the wallet auto-locks during the wait, the loop is gone.
   `ProcessTransactionData` survives in IndexedDB (`processTransactions`), but nothing picks it back
   up.

**The fallback that keeps it honest.** If auto-continue cannot run, the process should show
*"TAO arrived: tap to finish"* and the user signs leg 2 by hand. Funds never disappear; the worst
case is TAO sitting in the user's own wallet, which satisfies US-11.8's *resumable, never silently
lost* rule.

**Risks.** A single provider for leg 1: if SimpleSwap drops the TAO pair or dries up, the route
is gone. It is also a custodial desk, so leg 1 is not atomic. SimpleSwap's `fixed: true`
(guaranteed amount, wider spread, time-limited) could be offered as a user option.

**Unverified.** Whether SimpleSwap's TAO pair is live today. The status values and fields of
`GET /exchanges/{id}` through SubWallet's proxy (in particular, whether it returns the settled
amount). Whether `fixed: true` is offered for TAO.

#### Way 2 — TAO.com crosschain swap as a provider

**What it is.** Integrate TAO.com's route as one provider, the same shape as
[US-11.5](../sprints/stories/US-11.5-in-wallet-swap-via-simpleswap.md) (SimpleSwap) and
[US-11.9](../sprints/stories/US-11.9-in-wallet-swap-via-optimex.md) (Optimex). The wallet builds
**one** Ethereum transaction and TAO.com delivers dTAO.

**What has to be built.** A new handler under `swap-service/handler/`, a new `SwapProviderId`, and
backend support to emit the pair. Because it is a single SWAP action, it needs **no router
change and no One-sign change**, which is why it is the smallest engineering path once an API
exists.

**Blocked on.** An integration surface. No public API or SDK was found. The only secondary
coverage read ([KuCoin insight](https://www.kucoin.com/news/insight/USDC/6aad7d4f62cf37000742fc7f))
is commentary with no technical detail. **This is a partnership ask before it is an engineering
task.**

**Questions to ask TAO.com.** Is there a quote/order API, and on what terms (fees, rate limits,
referral share)? Is dTAO delivered as **native stake on the user's coldkey** (so it shows in
SubWallet's staking view) or as a token? Which subnets are covered? How are refunds and expiry
handled? Is there an order-status endpoint to track fills?

**A reference point, not a substitute.** TaoFi (above) is open source and covers the same user
journey with published contracts. It is a fallback if TAO.com has no API, but its output is an
ERC-20 alpha, not native stake, so it would need its own position view.

#### Way 3 — vTAO LayerZero OFT bridge (Ethereum ↔ Bittensor EVM)

**What vTAO is** (read from source, [latent-to/vtao](https://github.com/latent-to/vtao)). vTAO is a
**liquid-staking** token from TAO App, not a 1:1 wrapped TAO. `vTAO.sol` stakes everything to
netuid 0 (root) on one fixed hotkey. `stake(address)` (payable) mints vTAO and `unstake(uint256)`
burns it and returns TAO immediately on Bittensor EVM. The rate is read with `vTAOtoTAO()`. The
minimum is 0.0002 TAO per stake/unstake. It goes cross-chain as a LayerZero OFT: on Bittensor EVM
(chain 964) the token is `0x31049849CCdD983D8F401077537438eaF12B12Af` and the OFT adapter is
`0x79Ce842dEAA61D7668a2f31D964502F0d777bA89`, with OFTs on Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum and six more.
It was audited by Zellic ([ERC-7730 PR #3004](https://github.com/ethereum/clear-signing-erc7730-registry/pull/3004),
[LayerZero](https://x.com/LayerZero_Core/status/1977795028421574746)).

**Route.**

| # | Leg | Call | Exists? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Ethereum | USDC/ETH → vTAO on a DEX | Uniswap / Kyber handlers, **if a pool exists** |
| 2 | Ethereum → Bittensor EVM | `OFT.send()`, LayerZero fee in ETH from `quoteSend` | **No**, new bridge type in `balance-service/transfer/xcm/` |
| 3 | Bittensor EVM | `vTAO.unstake()` → TAO | **No**, new step type (neither SWAP nor BRIDGE) |
| 4 | Bittensor EVM → substrate | precompile `0x…0800` | Yes, `bittensorBridge` (1.3.78) |
| 5 | Substrate | TAO → alpha, `swapStakeLimit` / `addStakeLimit` | Yes, Bittensor handler |

The reverse direction runs 5 → 1, with `stake()` instead of `unstake()` at leg 3.

**Contracts and liquidity.** **No deploy.** OFT and adapter are live and audited; the wallet only
calls `send`, `stake`, `unstake`. **No liquidity for the bridge.** An OFT locks or burns on one
side and mints on the other, so there is no pool. **Leg 1 does depend on liquidity, but not
ours**: USDC → vTAO needs a pool on Ethereum. If it is thin the route is not worth using, and
SubWallet seeding it would make SubWallet a market maker carrying capital risk. The deeper vTAO
pools may be on Base or Arbitrum, in which case leg 1 belongs there.

**Also needed.** vTAO assets in the chain-list (source chain + Bittensor EVM). Delivery tracking
for LayerZero (LayerZero Scan or a destination balance delta). Everything Way 1 needs on the router
and One-sign side, because this route has two SWAP actions as well, plus a wait branch for the OFT
leg. And a **unified account**, since the route crosses EVM and substrate addresses. A single-type
Ledger account cannot run it.

**Unverified.** The Ethereum OFT address. vTAO pool depth on Ethereum/Base/Arbitrum. Actual
LayerZero delivery time on this pathway. **Nothing found says TAO.com uses vTAO.** Their post says
only *"Intents powered via LayerZero"*.

### Recommendation

**Pursue Way 2 as the target and build Way 1 as the in-house path, in parallel. Park Way 3.**

1. **Now, cheap, no code:** ask the backend team whether SimpleSwap's TAO pair is live and what
   `GET /exchanges/{id}` returns through the proxy. Ask BD to contact TAO.com with the questions
   under Way 2. Either answer can change the plan, so these come first.
2. **Way 2 if TAO.com has an API.** It is the only way with one signature and seconds to alpha,
   it needs no router or One-sign change, and it takes on no mid-route risk. It is the best user
   outcome for the least wallet code.
3. **Way 1 regardless.** It uses only providers SubWallet already ships. Its prerequisites
   (router SWAP→SWAP, an off-chain wait in One-sign, amount-received handoff, resume on restart) are
   **real defects in general multi-hop**, not route-specific work. They would also be needed by
   Way 3 and any future multi-swap route. Ship it with the *"tap to finish"* fallback before
   relying on auto-continue.
4. **Way 3 only if** Ways 1 and 2 both fail *and* a vTAO pool turns out deep enough. It is the
   longest route, with four points where funds can stop mid-route and two fee layers, for no UX
   gain over Way 1.

If this becomes work, Way 2 is a new provider story under EPIC-11, and Way 1's router and One-sign
work belongs on [US-11.8](../sprints/stories/US-11.8-cross-chain-swap-routing.md). The next section
still stands: none of this is scoped.
