## A. Research ask — USDC / ETH (Ethereum) ↔ dTAO

### The ask

Raised 2026-09-24: **look into swapping USDC or ETH on Ethereum directly into dTAO** (a Bittensor
subnet alpha token).

**There is no written specification behind it** — no issue, no requirement, nothing that states
what the route should do or who would carry it. Everything below is read out of this repository and
describes only what the code can and cannot do today. Nothing here is a requirement, and the ask
itself has not been checked against anything outside the repo
([LESSONS §69](../LESSONS.md): an unverified claim stays unverified until something corroborates it).

### The gap is one hop, and it is the middle one

A USDC(ethereum) → alpha route is, at minimum, **swap → bridge → swap**: sell USDC for something
bridgeable on Ethereum, move it to Bittensor, buy alpha with TAO. The first and third legs are
covered. **The middle leg does not exist in this tree.** Every bridge under
`balance-service/transfer/xcm/` was checked:

- **`bittensorBridge/`** — the closest, and it is not it. `getSubtensorEvmtoBittensorExtrinsic()`
  calls the precompile at `0x0000000000000000000000000000000000000800`, and the reverse direction is
  a substrate `balances.transferKeepAlive` to an `evmToSs58()` address. **Both legs are native TAO,
  between the Bittensor substrate chain and Subtensor EVM** — one ecosystem, one asset. It is
  #4901's bridge (1.3.78), not an Ethereum one.
- **`snowBridge.ts`** — the only Ethereum↔substrate bridge here, and it lands on Polkadot / Asset
  Hub, not Bittensor.
- **`acrossBridge/`, `polygonBridge.ts`, `posBridge.ts`, `availBridge.ts`** — EVM-side, none reaches
  Bittensor.

So today the router cannot build this route because no provider can quote the hop that leaves
Ethereum for Bittensor. That is the thing to research: **who carries value between Ethereum and
Bittensor, and on what terms.**

### Questions research has to answer, in order

1. **Is there a bridge at all?** Named, live, with an API the wallet could call. If the answer is a
   third-party swap desk rather than a bridge (the SimpleSwap / Optimex shape —
   [US-11.5](../sprints/stories/US-11.5-in-wallet-swap-via-simpleswap.md),
   [US-11.9](../sprints/stories/US-11.9-in-wallet-swap-via-optimex.md)), that is a **different
   integration** from a routed multi-hop and probably a smaller one.
2. **Which asset crosses?** TAO itself, or a wrapped/bridged representation? If the bridge lands
   TAO on Subtensor EVM rather than the substrate chain, `bittensorBridge` already covers the last
   leg and the route is **four hops**, not three.
3. **Does the router actually run three legs end to end?** `processStepsToPathActions()` builds
   `path` as a list, so nothing in the type structurally caps it at two — but US-11.8 was written
   and tested around **Swap→Bridge / Bridge→Swap**. Whether a third leg quotes, prices and resumes
   correctly is **unverified either way**; do not assume it does or does not.
4. **Where does the alpha end up?** `bittensor-handler.ts` reads `fromAsset.metadata?.netuid` and
   treats the native token as netuid 0, so the destination alpha has to be a chain-list asset with a
   `netuid`. A route into an arbitrary subnet needs that asset to exist first.
5. **What does it cost, and is it honest?** Three legs means three fees and two intermediate
   balances. [US-11.8](../sprints/stories/US-11.8-cross-chain-swap-routing.md)
   already carries the rule that a half-completed multi-hop must be resumable and never silently
   lost; a longer route makes that harder, not easier.

### Three ways

#### Way 1 — SimpleSwap (USDC/ETH → TAO) + Bittensor AMM (TAO → alpha)

#### Way 2 — TAO.com crosschain swap as a provider

#### Way 3 — vTAO LayerZero OFT bridge (Ethereum ↔ Bittensor EVM)

### Scope — not decided here

Nothing has been scoped and no story has been opened. If this becomes work, the shapes are:
**a new provider story under EPIC-11** (if a desk or bridge is integrated directly, next to US-11.5
/ US-11.9), or **routing work on US-11.8** (if the answer is a third leg through existing
providers), or **[US-11.17](../sprints/stories/US-11.17-swap-coverage-expansion.md)**,
`backlog`, which is where coverage expansion already lives. Picking one before question 1 is
answered would be inventing a requirement.

*Boundary: this is research, not a commitment. No FR is claimed, no AC is written, and nothing
outside this repository has been verified.*
